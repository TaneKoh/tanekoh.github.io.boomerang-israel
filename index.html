Introduction

My interest in the broader topic of the history of Palestinian oppression has been sparked by personal outrage over the ongoing genocide and ethnic cleansing, compounded by US complicity and support for the violence being enacted against the Palestinians. However, my specific interest in the frame of this paper refers to the connection between violent repressive techniques developed against Palestinians and how it mirrors American domestic policy and policing.

The "boomerang effect" is an established intellectual phenomenom developed by Aime Cesaire in Discourse on Colonialism and Hannah Arendt in The Origins of Totalitarianism. Both works articulate that the methods of domination used by European colonial powers inevitably returned to their mainland metropoles, where colonial administrative techniques would give rise to facism. Some examples of this include repressive techniques used against Vietnamese people in the Vietnam War then being brought back to the US in the form of modern policing, brutal counterterrorism tactics developed during the occupation of the Philippines then being replicated against political protesters back in the US, or Cesaire's original observation of the German-led genocide of the Herero, Nama, and San people of what is today Namibia being brought back to Europe in the form of the Holocaust.

1896

1917

Balfour Declaration is created. Britain promises a "Jewish national home" on Arab land.

1922

The League of Nations approves the British Mandate for Palestine and its purpose of helping establish a Jewish homeland.

1929

Al-Buraq uprising takes place, the first mass protests against increased Jewish immigration to Palestine.

1947

The UN passes Resolution 181, a partition plan for Palestine. Arab states and Palestinian leaders reject it.

1948

Israel declares independence, leading to the first Arab-Israeli war. Israel captures areas designated for the envisioned Palestinian state.

1948

During the ethnic cleansing of Palestinian Arabs by Zionists, often referred to as the Nakba, around 750,000 Palestinians were made to leave their homes and flee through various violent means. Dozens of massacres targeted Palestinian Arabs, and Israeli forces poisoned village wells with typhoid bacteria in violation of the 1925 Geneva protocols.

1964

Palestinian Liberation Organization (PLO) is founded.

1967

During the Six-Day War, Israel takes control of the West Bank, Gaza Strip, East Jerusalem, and Syria's Golan Heights and Egypt's Sinai Peninsula. The conflict displaces roughly 300,000 Palestinians.

1973

Known variously as the October War, Yom Kippur War, or Ramadan War, Egypt and Syria launch surprise attacks to reclaim Israeli-occupied areas. Organization of Arab Petroleum Exporting Countries begins an oil embargo on countries that support Israel, and the price of oil skyrockets. UNSC passes Resolution 338, calling for a ceasefire.

1978

Israel and Egypt sign the Camp David Accords, establishing diplomatic and commercial relations.

1987

The First Intifada is launched in occupied Palestine with roughly 200 Israelis and 1300 Palestinians killed over the next six years.

1988

Hamas founded.

1993

Israel and the PLO sign the Oslo Accords.

2000

Following failed negotations for a lasting peace agreement, the Second Intifada begins, resulting in roughly 3,000 Palestinian deaths and 1,000 Israeli deaths.

2002

Israel begins construction of the West Bank Barrier to protect Israeli towns and settlements, cutting off some Palestinians' access to work and schooling.

2008

After nearly 800 rocket attacks from Gaza on southern Israeli towns, Israel launches a military campaign in the Gaza Strip, and in less than a month 1,400 Palestinians and 13 Israelis are killed.

2014

After Hamas militants kidnap and murder three Israeli teenagers, Israel launches an invasion of Gaza, named Operation Protective Edge. Over the next 50 days, roughly 2,000 Palestinians and 71 Israelis are killed.

2022

After years of escalating conflict and failed peace plans from the Trump Administration, Israel launches Operation Break the Wave, a campaign of near-daily counterterrorism raids in the West Bank. By the end of the year, Israeli forces have killed roughly 170 Palestinians and Palestinian forces have killed 29 Israelis.

2023

In the deadliest attack on Israel, Hamas launches a surprise attack, killing 1,200 Israelis and taking 251 hostages. Israel responds immediately by launching air strikes, and over the next 2 years, tens of thousands of Palestinians are killed, while Israel loses about a thousand soldiers.

2024

2025

Israel and Hamas agree to a ceasefire, but within months both sides accuse each other of violating the agreement, and Israel launches renewed air strikes and blockades and resumes full-scale ground operations.

2026

International Connections

In the first layer of international connections, post-9/11 national security framing has exacerbated a demand for weapons technology, empwering the military industrial complex and encouraging demand for Israeli weapons firms and training program supply tools. This is specifically exemplified in Israeli military technology exports through firms such as Cellebrite and Cognyte, [], as well as through training exchange programs like the ADL, GILEE, amongst others.

The ADL is a self-proclaimed "civil rights" agency that has worked to train law enforcement professionals across the country, and has a long history of surveilling, blacklisting, and targeting racial justice and Palestinian activists. The Anti-Defamation League (ADL) facilitates US police trainings in Israel through the National Counter-Terrorism Seminar, sending hundreds of American officials to Israel since 2003.

JINSA is an American-Israeli security-oriented think tank that advocates for increased military spending and Israeli security partnerships. They launched their Law Enforcement Exchange Program (LEEP) in 2002 shortly after 9/11. Since then, over 11,000 American law enforcement officials have attended LEEP conferences, where Israeli security officials are held as experts.

Groups like the ADL, JINSA, and GILEE run counter to civil rights principles and movements, upholding Israel's Apartheid regime as a model for US law enforcement, while their policing and legal systems are based explicitly on racial differentiation. The exchange programs facilitate the sharing of practices and technologies between US law enforcement and Israeli military, police, and intelligence agencies. Militarized approaches to security are then brought back to the US, normalizing mass surveillance, criminalization, and violent repression of communities and movements deemed threatening, highlighting a security model that deems human and civil rights violations necessary.

Local-level connections

On a local level, as police departments adopt these invasive technologies, they are translated to street-level surveillance and enforcement programs that disproportionately target racial minorities. The invasive cell phone surveillance and phone extraction practices are used to suppress protest and dissent, and are used in conjunction with racial and ethnic profiling in so-called "counter-terrorism" practices.

Reports from Amnesty International and Surveillance Technology Oversight Project (S.T.O.P.) have documented how these "counter-terrorism" practices are repurposed for general policing, but specifically used to target protesters and produce distrust in surveilled communities.

There are a multitude of reports and examples of local democratic processes being superceded by local officials or law enforcement agencies when it comes to the use and purchases of these invasive technologies.
